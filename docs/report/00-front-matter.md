# Abstract

Generic workout and diet plans that are made for everyone ignore the individual characteristics of each person, so they are rarely the best choice for a specific user and a specific goal. Beginners also train in the gym without any feedback on their technique, which lowers their confidence and raises the risk of injury. FitNova is an AI fitness platform that tries to close these gaps by joining three connected modules in one system: a personalized workout planner, an exercise form-correction module, and a nutrition guidance module.

The aim was to build every module on a published dataset and a published method, so the work can be compared in a fair and honest way against prior research instead of relying on guesswork. The workout planner is a three-layer pipeline. A content-based filter scores a catalogue of 3,048 programs against the user profile, a Neural Collaborative Filtering model (NeuMF) re-ranks the best candidates, and a four-agent language model crew assembles a grounded seven-day plan that only uses real exercises from the dataset. The form-correction module was rebuilt from scratch on the Fitness-AQA dataset and method, after an earlier pose-based approach was found not to transfer to real video. Video errors such as the knees caving inward during a squat are detected with an R(2+1)D-18 three-dimensional convolutional network trained with Motion-Disentangling self-supervised pretraining, and a single-image depth error is detected with a ResNet-18 trained with pose-contrastive pretraining. The nutrition module computes calorie and macronutrient targets with a deterministic engine that guarantees correctness, selects recipes from a cleaned Food.com pool, and uses a four-agent crew that only annotates the plan and never changes the numbers or invents food. A separate DistilGPT-2 model reproduces a recipe instruction generation task taken from the literature.

The system matches or beats the methods it was built on. On the official Fitness-AQA test splits the form models reach a higher macro F1 than the published method on all three exercises (Squat 0.6304 against 0.6262, overhead press 0.6622 against 0.6502, shallow squat 0.8902 against 0.8694), and an independent overfitting audit confirms that the deployed models generalize. The recommender reaches HR@10 of 0.9270 and NDCG@10 of 0.7469. The DistilGPT-2 generator beats all three learned models of the reference paper on the BLEU, ROUGE and Distinct text-quality scores. The numbers are reported with their limits stated openly: the form models are measured on an in-the-wild benchmark and are still weak on casual front-facing phone video, the recommender is trained on synthetic interaction data, and the recipe generator improves the quality of the text rather than the personalization that was the original paper's main contribution.
